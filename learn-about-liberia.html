The Nation

A Nation Rebuilding from the Ruins of War

Liberia is a small nation of approximately 5 million people situated on West Africa's Atlantic coast — a country of extraordinary resilience forged through extraordinary suffering.

Liberia endured a brutal 14-year civil war from 1989 to 2003 that decimated entire communities, destroyed school buildings, drove away teachers, and collapsed the infrastructure needed to educate a generation. When the guns fell silent, an entire generation of children had grown up without ever sitting in a classroom.

The scars of that war are still visible today — in crumbling schoolhouses, in communities without teachers, and most painfully, in families where no adult can read or write. Rebuilding a literate, educated nation does not happen overnight. It requires people who care deeply enough to act — people like you.

The Human Cost

The Impact on Children

Behind every statistic is a child's face, a family's dream, and a future waiting to be unlocked.

🏘️

No Access to Schooling

Many children in rural areas of Liberia have zero access to any school. There are no buildings, no teachers, and no path to literacy without organizations like CEL stepping in.

💔

Generational Poverty

Families in rural Margibi County — where Gifts of Hope School is located — face extreme poverty. Without education, the cycle continues from parent to child to grandchild, unbroken.

👧

Girls at Greatest Risk

Girls are especially vulnerable. Cultural pressures often keep them home to work instead of attending school, compounding the already devastating gap in female literacy.

🔗

The Cycle Continues

An uneducated population cannot fully rebuild a nation. Every child denied schooling today is a potential leader, teacher, or healer lost to Liberia's future tomorrow.

The need is urgent — but there is real hope. In rural Kakata, Gifts of Hope Community School is breaking this cycle one child at a time. 136 children are already in classrooms who would otherwise have none.

Hope in Action

How CEL Is Responding

When Harry Gorwor saw children in the rural areas around Kakata with no path to education, he did not look away. He built a school.

On September 8, 2018, Gifts of Hope Community School opened its doors in rural Kakata, Margibi County — reaching children who had never sat in a classroom, held a pencil, or heard the name of Jesus in a lesson.

Today, 136 children attend Gifts of Hope, supported by a team of 35 dedicated educators and staff. They learn to read, to write, to do math — and they experience the transforming love of Christ. This is what CEL makes possible through your generosity.
